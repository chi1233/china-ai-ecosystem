# China AI Ecosystem Map

**Capstone Research · Interactive Evidence Map**  
Chinonso Anyanwu · Schwarzman College, Tsinghua University · 2026

[**View the live site →**](https://china-ai-ecosystem.vercel.app/)

## What This Is

An interactive research map of 40 selected entities in China’s frontier AI ecosystem. The dataset includes private companies, public companies, corporate AI units, infrastructure providers, research institutions, and capital actors.

The project examines how public policy, capital, compute infrastructure, universities, research institutions, and firms interact within China’s AI ecosystem. It supports a capstone on **developmental orchestration**, with Beijing as the primary case and Hangzhou as a contrastive reference.

This is a purposive research sample. It is not a company ranking, a complete national census, or a causal estimate of AI Pilot Zone effects.

## Research Question

How does China operationalize artificial intelligence as a prospective comparative advantage through state-structured coordination mechanisms, particularly AI Pilot Zones?

## Core Argument

> China’s AI Pilot Zones can be understood as instruments of developmental orchestration: institutional arrangements through which public authorities attempt to coordinate capital, compute and data infrastructure, research institutions, firms, and commercialization pathways.

The capstone evaluates whether this coordination framework extends New Structural Economics into the digital economy. It does not assume that ecosystem outcomes were caused by Pilot Zone designation. Policy attribution requires evidence of implementation, firm participation, sequencing, and plausible counterfactuals.

## Selected Findings

- **Sample concentration:** Beijing contains 21 of the 40 selected entities. Beijing, Hangzhou, and Shanghai together contain 33, or 82.5%.
- **Institutional density:** Beijing combines major universities, public research institutions, AI firms, platform companies, capital providers, and policy bodies within one regional ecosystem.
- **Contrasting coordination models:** Beijing is examined as a state-academic and research-intensive ecosystem, while Hangzhou provides a contrastive case centered more heavily on platform firms, municipal initiatives, Zhejiang University, and Zhijiang Lab.
- **Infrastructure relevance:** Domestic accelerator and compute providers are included because compute access and semiconductor capacity shape China’s AI development environment. Inclusion does not establish that policy support caused their commercial outcomes.
- **University relationships:** The dataset distinguishes formal spinouts, institutional partnerships, research affiliations, founder education, and general alumni relationships. These categories are not treated as interchangeable.
- **Causal restraint:** Observed proximity among government programs, institutions, investors, and firms is evidence of ecosystem structure. It is not by itself proof that a particular policy produced a firm-level outcome.

## Quantitative Notes

- Tsinghua University recorded **4,986 AI and machine-learning patents between 2005 and 2024**, according to LexisNexis data reported by Bloomberg and Fortune.
- Startup Genome data reported for 2023–2024 indicate that approximately **66.2% of Beijing ecosystem startup funding went to AI-native companies**. This is a funding-concentration measure, not Beijing’s share of global AI investment.
- AI World reports approximately **US$4.4 billion in Beijing AI investment during 2025**, compared with approximately US$1.1 billion in Shanghai. This is a third-party estimate rather than an official municipal expenditure total.
- Talent-origin statistics depend on the cohort and definition used. **The Economist’s March 2026 analysis of a random sample of 600 accepted NeurIPS 2025 papers, covering almost 4,000 authors, found that 51% of presenting researchers began their careers in China, operationalized through undergraduate origins; this was up from 29% in 2019 using MacroPolo’s method.** MacroPolo’s separate 2024 tracker reports 38% for its own cohort, so the figures should not be treated as directly interchangeable.

## Evidence Standards

The evidence register applies the following source hierarchy:

1. Regulatory filings, prospectuses, annual reports, exchange announcements, government documents, and official institutional statements.
2. Company announcements and official investor-relations materials.
3. Established news organizations and specialist publications with identifiable reporting.
4. Secondary databases and ecosystem reports, used with explicit qualifications.
5. Unverified claims, promotional descriptions, and unsourced relationship assertions are excluded or marked as requiring verification.

A relationship is treated as verified only when the available evidence identifies the relevant institutions or actors and supports the specific relationship category assigned. Founder education, alumni status, partnership, incubation, investment, and formal university spinout status are coded separately.

## Methodological Limits

The 40 entities were selected for analytical relevance to the capstone’s mechanism domains. The dataset therefore overrepresents prominent AI hubs and organizations with publicly accessible evidence.

The map cannot independently establish:

- That AI Pilot Zone designation caused firm formation, financing, innovation, or commercialization.
- That firms located in a Pilot Zone received or benefited from a particular policy instrument.
- That observed institutional relationships are stronger than comparable relationships outside the selected cities.
- That the selected entities represent the full population of Chinese AI organizations.
- That qualitative ecosystem or pipeline assessments constitute standardized performance indices.

Causal claims require additional evidence, including policy implementation records, firm-level participation, funding or subsidy data, temporal sequencing, interviews, and counterfactual comparison.

## Theoretical Grounding

|---|---|
| New Structural Economics | Provides the underlying account of comparative advantage, structural upgrading, and facilitating-state coordination. |
| Developmental orchestration | Identifies the proposed mechanism connecting public coordination to capital, infrastructure, institutional integration, and commercialization. |
| Developmental-state theory | Provides comparison with established accounts of state capacity, industrial policy, and public-private coordination. |
| Entrepreneurial-state theory | Helps evaluate public risk absorption, market formation, and mission-oriented investment. |
| Innovation-systems literature | Provides alternative explanations based on regional institutions, knowledge networks, firms, and research organizations. |
